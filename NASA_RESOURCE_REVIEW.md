# NASA visual integration

Earth Radio uses sources from the nine visualization families Chris supplied. This does **not** mean every dataset, pollutant, frame, or camera angle on those pages is included. Twelve curated listening stations use nine distinct NASA sources as pixel-derived **sound inputs**. GEDI forest height is a sound-only input, not a selectable view. Four additional video excerpts are **visual context only**. The source window shows the original NASA frame for selectable views; when a suitable flat map is selected, its frames also become the globe's primary texture. Changing the selected view alone does not change the station's multi-source sound mix.

The [EIC content gallery](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/gallery/earth-information-center/) is an index of more visuals, not an additional source layer. [Blue Marble](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/2915/) is the base globe texture, not a sound input. All station pins are editorial entry points, not sensor locations. Sources run on independent historical timelines and their pixels are not calibrated physical measurements. See the [README](README.md) for per-source credits, musical mappings, rights and setup. NASA does not endorse Earth Radio.

## Current detailed media for all visualization types

All 13 source types retain detailed desktop media; GEDI is used for sound only. The globe maps use 2048–4096-pixel video; the aerosol still is the native 7680 × 4320 NASA image; GEDI and CO₂ use 3840 × 2160 video; the four camera-context excerpts use 1920 × 1080. The smaller copies now drive sound analysis on every device. Detailed view is optional for one selected display at a time, with fallback to the standard view if it fails. Scene comparisons against the previous desktop versions passed at three points in every video. See [the current resolution table](VISUAL_RESOLUTION.md) and `visual-resolution.json` for source URLs, dimensions, hashes, and excerpt offsets. The following sections describe earlier resolution passes.

## Earlier display resolution pass

The original [3912 ocean](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3912/), [5505 Perpetual Ocean 2](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5505/), and [5475 Black Marble](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5475/) downloads were verified at 2048 × 1024. Earth Radio now bundles optimized 1280 × 640 desktop excerpts alongside the existing 640 × 320 ocean and ocean2 copies and 960 × 480 Black Marble copy. Wide, capable displays use the sharper versions; mobile and data-saving clients use the smaller versions. This does not add geographic precision, a new dataset, or a calibrated signal. The original EIC night-light left panel and several other bundled visualization clips remain at their previous lower resolution.

## Earlier flat-map resolution pass

All seven source families currently draped on the globe now have higher-detail authentic NASA options for capable screens. The 1280 × 640 ocean copies come from SVS [3912](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3912/) and [5505](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5505/); the 1280 × 640 Black Marble copy comes from [5475](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5475/). The EIC annual night-light left panel increased from 640 × 360 to 1280 × 720, cut from the two-panel 7680 × 2160 source at [5313](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5313/). The historical ISS lightning and PM2.5 model clips increased from 640 × 320 to 1280 × 640, from [5011](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5011/) and [5518](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5518/). The single 2025 aerosol still increased from 1920 × 1080 to its official 3840 × 2160 version at [5669](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5669/). Native frames are resampled and compressed for browser delivery, without generative upscaling. The source window keeps the unmasked NASA imagery; the Black Marble year-caption mask applies only to its globe texture.

Desktop WebGL now uses the native 5400 × 2700 [August 2004 Blue Marble Next Generation map with topography and bathymetry](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-topography-bathymetry/), when the GPU supports that texture size. The sphere renders near its displayed pixel size, capped at 2048, and overlay sampling preserves available source detail up to 4096 × 2048. Existing videos are not enlarged. The original 2048 × 1024 Blue Marble image remains for narrow, data-saving, low-resource or unsupported clients and failed downloads. Those lean clients retain their 640-pixel sphere and 640 × 320 overlay map. The Canvas 2D fallback keeps its previous limits and a bounded software copy of the base map. The EIC night-light and aerosol presentation images remain **approximate** drapes because they are not pixel-registered to a 2:1 globe projection. More pixels do not improve geographic accuracy or create new physical measurements. Moving-camera films remain in the adjacent source window. Physical phone performance remains to be measured.
