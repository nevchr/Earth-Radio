# NASA visual integration

Earth Radio now offers an in-app view for **all nine visualization families Chris supplied**. This does **not** mean every dataset, pollutant, frame, or camera angle on those pages is included. Twelve curated listening stations use nine distinct NASA sources as pixel-derived **sound inputs**. Four additional video excerpts are **visual context only**. The source window always shows the original NASA frame; when a suitable flat map is selected, its frames also become the globe's primary texture. The selected globe view does not change the station's multi-source sound mix.

The [EIC content gallery](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/gallery/earth-information-center/) is an index of more visuals, not an additional source layer. [Blue Marble](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/2915/) is the base globe texture, not a sound input. All station pins are editorial entry points, not sensor locations. Sources run on independent historical timelines and their pixels are not calibrated physical measurements. See the [README](README.md) for per-source credits, musical mappings, rights and setup. NASA does not endorse Earth Radio.

## Display resolution pass

The original [3912 ocean](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3912/), [5505 Perpetual Ocean 2](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5505/), and [5475 Black Marble](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5475/) downloads were verified at 2048 × 1024. Earth Radio now bundles optimized 1280 × 640 desktop excerpts alongside the existing 640 × 320 ocean and ocean2 copies and 960 × 480 Black Marble copy. Wide, capable displays use the sharper versions; mobile and data-saving clients use the smaller versions. This does not add geographic precision, a new dataset, or a calibrated signal. The original EIC night-light left panel and several other bundled visualization clips remain at their previous lower resolution.

## Complete flat-map resolution pass

All seven source families currently draped on the globe now have higher-detail authentic NASA options for capable screens. The 1280 × 640 ocean copies come from SVS [3912](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3912/) and [5505](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5505/); the 1280 × 640 Black Marble copy comes from [5475](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5475/). The EIC annual night-light left panel increased from 640 × 360 to 1280 × 720, cut from the two-panel 7680 × 2160 source at [5313](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5313/). The historical ISS lightning and PM2.5 model clips increased from 640 × 320 to 1280 × 640, from [5011](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5011/) and [5518](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5518/). The single 2025 aerosol still increased from 1920 × 1080 to its official 3840 × 2160 version at [5669](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5669/). Native frames are resampled and compressed for browser delivery, without generative upscaling. The source window keeps the unmasked NASA imagery; the Black Marble year-caption mask applies only to its globe texture.

The globe samples desktop sources into a map up to 1536 × 768 and renders its sphere near the displayed pixel size, capped at 1024. Narrow, data-saving, and reported low-resource clients select the retained small source files and a 640 × 320 sample map. The EIC night-light and aerosol presentation images remain **approximate** drapes because their page imagery is not pixel-registered to a 2:1 globe projection. Higher pixel count does not improve geographic accuracy or create new physical measurements. The moving-camera GEDI, CO₂, river, and ocean close-up films are not flat maps; their images stay in the adjacent source window. Blue Marble remains 2048 × 1024, enough for the globe renderer’s 1024-pixel cap. Physical phone performance remains to be measured.
